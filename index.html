Questions 1–4

Relationships and ratios

1Additive or Multiplicative? Which table shows an ADDITIVE relationship?

Multiplicative means you multiply the top by the same number to get the bottom every time.

Worked solution

D. The top goes up by 2 and the bottom goes up by 2. Each bottom number is the top number plus 5, so y = x + 5. That is additive.

A multiplies by 4 (1×4 = 4, 3×4 = 12). B multiplies by 3. C multiplies by 5. Those three are multiplicative.

2A school orders 4 blue notebooks for every 7 red notebooks. Select TWO ratios equivalent to 4:7.

Multiply both numbers by the same number.

Worked solution

B and C. 4×3 = 12 and 7×3 = 21, so 12:21. 4×4 = 16 and 7×4 = 28, so 16:28.

A (8:12) and D (20:30) do not use the same multiplier for both numbers. E (7:4) flips the ratio.

3A fruit punch recipe uses 3 cups of orange juice for every 5 cups of pineapple juice. How many cups of pineapple juice are needed to make 40 cups of punch?

How many cups are in one batch of punch?

Worked solution

C. 25 cups. One batch is 3 + 5 = 8 cups. 40 ÷ 8 = 5 batches. Pineapple juice is 5 × 5 = 25 cups.

4A team has 2 seventh-graders for every 3 eighth-graders. Which table is correct?

Step 1: Start from 2 seventh-graders for every 3 eighth-graders. Step 2: Multiply both numbers by 2, then by 3, then by 4. Step 3: Pick the table that shows those pairs. Adding a different amount each time does not keep the ratio.

Worked solution

A. (2, 3), (4, 6), (6, 9), (8, 12). Both numbers are multiplied by 2, then 3, then 4.

B, (2, 3), (4, 5), (6, 8), (8, 11), does not keep the 2 to 3 ratio.

Questions 5–8

Tables, equations, and totals

5Which situation matches?

x24610
y6121830

Divide a bottom number by the top number above it. The situation should use that same “for every” relationship.

Worked solution

A. y = 3x. 2×3 = 6, 4×3 = 12, 6×3 = 18, and 10×3 = 30. Mia earns $3 for every $1 Jay earns.

6Group A: rectangle length is 6 units greater than width. Group B: length is 3 times width. Write an equation for each (width = x, length = y). Which is MULTIPLICATIVE? Explain.

Which group is MULTIPLICATIVE?

“Greater than” means add. “Times” means multiply.

Worked solution

Group A: y = x + 6. y = 6 + x means the same thing. The length is 6 more than the width, so this one adds.

Group B: y = 3x. y = 3*x and y = x*3 mean the same thing.

Group B is multiplicative because you multiply the width by 3 every time.

7A bakery uses 2 cups of flour for 16 muffins. How many cups for 80 muffins?

Flour (cups)0246?
Muffins016324880

Step 1: See how many equal groups of muffins fit into the total. Step 2: Each group uses the same cups of flour as the first pair. Step 3: Multiply the number of groups by the flour in one group. The answer is the flour, not the number of groups.

Worked solution

10 cups. 80 ÷ 16 = 5 groups of muffins. 5 × 2 = 10 cups of flour.

8Box A: 2 chocolate cookies for every 5 sugar cookies. Box B: 3 peanut butter cookies for every 4 sugar cookies. If EACH box has 20 sugar cookies, which box has more TOTAL cookies? Show your work and explain.

Which box has more total cookies?

Each box has 20 sugar cookies. Scale the other cookies, then add them to 20.

Worked solution

Box A: 20 ÷ 5 = 4 groups, so chocolate cookies = 2 × 4 = 8. Total = 8 + 20 = 28.

Box B: 20 ÷ 4 = 5 groups, so peanut butter cookies = 3 × 5 = 15. Total = 15 + 20 = 35.

Box B has more total cookies, because 35 is more than 28.

Rates, percent, and a bigger bag

9Movie tickets cost $6 each. Complete the table, graph the ordered pairs on your paper, and write an equation for y in terms of x.

Tickets (x) 12358
Cost (y)

Each ticket costs $6. Multiply the number of tickets by 6.

Worked solution

The costs are 6, 12, 18, 30, and 48. The equation is y = 6x. y = 6*x and y = x*6 mean the same thing.

On your paper, plot (1, 6), (2, 12), (3, 18), (5, 30), and (8, 48). The points line up on a straight line through (0, 0).

10A student reads 120 pages in 40 minutes at a constant rate. Complete the table. What is the unit rate in pages per minute?

Time (min) 510204060
Pages 120

Divide 120 pages by 40 minutes. That is the pages in one minute.

Worked solution

Pages: 15, 30, 60, 120, 180. The unit rate is 3 pages per minute, because 120 ÷ 40 = 3. Then 5×3 = 15, 10×3 = 30, 20×3 = 60, and 60×3 = 180.

11A grid has 100 equal squares. 65 are shaded. What percent is shaded?

The grid has 100 squares. The number shaded is the percent.

Worked solution

C. 65%. 65 shaded squares out of 100 is 65%.

12A bag has 5 red, 4 blue and 3 green marbles. A larger bag has 60 marbles in the SAME ratio. How many are green?

Add the red, blue, and green marbles first. Then see how many of those groups fit in 60.

Worked solution

B. 15 green marbles. 5 + 4 + 3 = 12 marbles in the small bag. 60 ÷ 12 = 5 groups. Green is 3 × 5 = 15.

Questions 1–4

Fractions, decimals, and percents

1Write the fraction 3/8 as a decimal.

Divide the numerator by the denominator. Keep dividing until the remainder is 0. Placing a decimal point between the two digits of the fraction is not dividing.

Worked solution

0.375. Divide 3 by 8. 8 goes into 30 three times (24), with remainder 6. 8 goes into 60 seven times (56), with remainder 4. 8 goes into 40 five times exactly. So 3/8 = 0.375.

0.38 writes the digits of the fraction with a decimal point. 0.3 stops after the tenths place. 3.8 puts the decimal point in the wrong place.

2Write the decimal 0.45 as a percent.

To change a decimal to a percent, move the decimal point two places to the right. Then write the percent sign.

Worked solution

45%. Move the decimal point in 0.45 two places to the right: 45. Then write the percent sign. 0.45 = 45%.

4.5% moves the point only one place. 0.45% does not move it. 450% moves it three places.

3Write 40% as a fraction in simplest form.

Write the percent as a fraction with a denominator of 100. Divide the numerator and the denominator by the same number until they have no common factor left. An equal fraction that is not fully simplified does not count.

Worked solution

2/5. 40% means 40/100. Divide the numerator and the denominator by 20: 40 ÷ 20 = 2 and 100 ÷ 20 = 5. 2 and 5 have no common factor other than 1, so 2/5 is in simplest form.

4/10 and 40/100 are equal to 40%, but both can still be simplified. 4/5 = 80%, not 40%.

4Write the fraction 7/10 as a percent.

Divide the numerator by the denominator to make a decimal. Move that decimal point two places to the right and add a percent sign.

Worked solution

70%. 7 ÷ 10 = 0.7. Move the decimal point two places to the right: 70. So 7/10 = 70%.

7% forgets to move the decimal point. 0.7% puts a percent sign on the decimal without moving the point. 17% adds the digits of the fraction.

Questions 5–8

Small percents, large percents, and repeating decimals

5Write 4% as a decimal.

To change a percent to a decimal, move the decimal point two places to the left. A one-digit percent needs a zero in the tenths place.

Worked solution

0.04. 4% means 4 hundredths. Start at 4.0 and move the decimal point two places to the left: 0.04.

0.4 is 40%, because the point moved only one place. 4 is 400%. 0.004 is 0.4%, because the point moved three places.

6Write 125% as a decimal.

A percent greater than 100 is greater than 1 whole. Move the decimal point two places to the left.

Worked solution

1.25. 125% is more than 100%, so the decimal is more than 1. Move the decimal point two places to the left: 125.0 becomes 1.25. 125% = 125/100 = 1.25.

0.125 is 12.5%. 12.5 is 1,250%. 125 leaves the percent as a whole number.

7Write 1/3 as a decimal.

Divide the numerator by the denominator. If the same remainder comes back every step, that digit repeats. Draw a bar over the repeating digit.

Worked solution

0.3 with a bar over the 3. Divide 1 by 3. 3 goes into 10 three times, remainder 1. The remainder is 1 again, so the digit 3 repeats forever: 0.333... A bar over the 3 shows that it repeats.

A plain 0.3 stops after one digit. 0.13 writes the digits of the fraction. 3.0 divides 3 by 1.

8Write 2/3 as a decimal.

Divide the numerator by the denominator and watch the remainder. If it never becomes 0, the decimal repeats. Draw a bar over the digit that keeps returning. Rounding is a different number.

Worked solution

0.6 with a bar over the 6. Divide 2 by 3. 3 goes into 20 six times, remainder 2. The remainder is 2 again, so the digit 6 repeats forever: 0.666...

A plain 0.6 is not marked as repeating. 0.67 rounds the repeating decimal and is a little too big. 0.23 writes the digits of the fraction.

Questions 9–12

Compare and word problems

9Which list orders 1/4, 0.3, 28%, and 2/5 from least to greatest?

Change every value into a decimal before you compare. For a percent, move the decimal point two places to the left. Then put the original labels in the same order as those decimals, from least to greatest.

Worked solution

1/4, 28%, 0.3, 2/5. Write each value as a decimal. 1/4 = 0.25. 28% = 0.28. 0.3 = 0.30. 2/5 = 0.40. From least to greatest those decimals are 0.25, 0.28, 0.30, 0.40.

10A team won 18 of 24 games. What percent of its games did the team win?

Write wins over games played as a fraction. Simplify by dividing the top and the bottom by the same number. Then convert that fraction to a percent. The number of wins alone is not the percent.

Worked solution

75%. 18 wins out of 24 games is 18/24. Divide the numerator and the denominator by 6: 3/4. 3 ÷ 4 = 0.75, and 0.75 = 75%.

18% uses only the number of wins. 67% is about 2/3, and 18/24 is not 2/3. 80% is 4/5.

11A $40 shirt is 25% off. What fraction of the original price is taken off, in simplest form?

A percent is that many parts out of 100. Write it as a fraction over 100 and simplify. The dollar price is not the denominator.

Worked solution

1/4. 25% = 25/100. Divide the numerator and the denominator by 25: 1/4. One fourth of the price is taken off.

1/25 puts 25 in the denominator only. 25/40 uses the dollar price as the denominator. 2/5 = 40%, not 25%.

12A $40 shirt is 25% off. What is the sale price?

Turn the percent into a decimal and multiply by the original price. That product is the discount. Subtract the discount from the original price to get the sale price.

Worked solution

$30. 25% = 0.25. The discount is 0.25 × 40 = 10 dollars. The sale price is 40 − 10 = 30 dollars.

$10 is only the discount. $15 subtracts 25 dollars instead of 25 percent (40 − 25 = 15). $35 takes off only 5 dollars.
